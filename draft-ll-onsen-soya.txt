     Service Orchestration with YANG-modeled Abstractions Framework
                       draft-ll-onsen-soya-latest

Abstract

   This document defines the Service Orchestration with YANG-modeled
   Abstractions (SOYA) Framework for building layered orchestration
   systems using YANG models, and specifies the interactions between
   different orchestration layers.

1.  Introduction

   The YANG Data Modeling Language ([RFC7950]) has become ubiquitous in
   modern network management and is widely adopted for modeling
   configuration and state data for network devices.  YANG equally lends
   itself to describing higher-level orchestration intent at various
   layers of abstraction.

   This document defines the Service Orchestration with YANG-modeled
   Abstractions (SOYA) Framework for building layered orchestration
   systems.  Each layer allows for the expression of a service intent,
   according to a set of YANG models.  These layers may be hosted on
   separate SOYA Orchestrators or within a single SOYA Orchestrator,
   depending on the deployment architecture.

   Service intent, e.g. configuration, flows from the highest
   abstraction layer down to the managed devices.  Operational state, on
   the other hand, flows from the devices up to the higher abstraction
   layers.  The framework specifies permissible data exchanges between
   different abstraction layers, both within a single SOYA Orchestrator
   and across multiple SOYA Orchestrators.

1.1.  Definition of SOYA Orchestrator

   In the context of this document, a SOYA Orchestrator refers to a
   software component responsible for managing network services by
   processing service intent and operational state.  A variety of terms
   exist in the industry, including domain orchestrator, network
   orchestrator, domain controller, and SDN controller.  Any platform
   capable of adhering to the SOYA framework, can be referred to as a
   SOYA Orchestrator.

2.  The SOYA Orchestrator

   A SOYA Orchestrator is a software component capable of receiving,
   storing, and processing service intent.  In a SOYA Orchestrator,
   intent is stored in a datastore as defined in [RFC7950].  SOYA
   Orchestrators must implement, at a minimum, the <running-config>
   datastore as defined in Section 5.1.3 of [RFC8342].

   Processing each received intent update involves executing the SOYA
   Orchestrator's business logic.  Typically, this will result in a
   corresponding output intent for next orchestration layer down; or for
   the managed device(s) in case of the lowest service layer.

   The SOYA Orchestrator may also support receiving/collecting
   operational state from its managed devices or from the service layer
   below.  Upon receiving operational state updates, the SOYA
   Orchestrator should persist them in its datastore.  Processing each
   received operational state update involves analyzing it according to
   its business logic and determining the necessary actions.  Those may
   include updating the SOYA Orchestrator's internal state, generating
   new service intents for the lower layer, or triggering specific
   configuration changes on managed devices.  The SOYA Orchestrator can
   also propagate operational state updates to the next orchestration
   layer up.  The SOYA Orchestrator can also generate notifications or
   raise alarms.

2.1.  Single-Layer Orchestrator

   A single-layer SOYA Orchestrator is responsible for managing network
   services at a specific layer of abstraction.  It processes service
   intents and operational state relevant to its layer and interacts
   with both the higher orchestration layer (if any) and the lower
   orchestration layer or managed devices.

2.2.  Multi-Layer Orchestrator

   A multi-layer SOYA Orchestrator is capable of managing network
   services on multiple layers of abstraction within a single SOYA
   Orchestrator.

3.  Service Layering

   An overall orchestration system may be comprised of multiple layers,
   each responsible for a specific level of abstraction in managing
   network services.  The schema for each layer is defined by a set of
   YANG models.
